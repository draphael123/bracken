# THE CHURCH — optional level after Waymeet, the Paladin as its boss

Daniel, 2026-09-20: an optional level after Waymeet that unlocks the Paladin, with priest enemies who heal
their allies and throw light bolts. Structure and names agreed below.

## The names

Waymeet's boss (`closedhelm`) is displayed today as **THE PALADIN**, "sworn to the chapel" - which is the
name the class takes. He is renamed **THE CRUSADER**, and the church's own boss is **THE PALADIN**, so the
mirror is explicit: you beat the Paladin to buy the Paladin. His Waymeet subtitle already points at a chapel,
so the optional level is the place he was sworn to.

Rename touches: the bestiary card, `BEAST_SHORT`, the boss plate (the "THE PALADIN  THE WARD IS DOWN" and
"THE PALADIN  HIS OATH" lines in main.js), the boss-rush entry, and any sign text in Waymeet that names him.

## What makes it unique: the light is the level

A two-state church. **Consecrated light is the clergy's power**: in lit rooms the priests heal harder, their
bolts hit harder, and more of the congregation is awake. **In the dark the clergy are weak - but the dark is
what the church has been keeping down**, and the crypt's tenants come up into it.

So every room asks the player a question no other level asks: *fight the priests in their light, or fight the
dark's things without it.* The player controls which enemy set they face, and pays either way.

Nothing else in the campaign does this. Highcrown is castle combat, the Mage's Folly is a puzzle tower, the
Burial Caverns is a catacomb; none of them has a world with two states and a switch in the player's hand.

## The structure: a rite that raises the danger (agreed)

The shape is a cruciform church, which gives the verticality the audit asks for without inventing anything:

- **The nave** - the long hall, the level's spine.
- **Two transept chapels** off it, each with a lamp.
- **The crypt** below, dark, where the things the light holds down are.
- **The organ gallery** above, reached from the transepts.
- **The sanctuary**, behind a barred rood screen at the east end: the boss.

**The rood screen opens to a rite: three chapel lamps lit.** And each lamp lit makes the church brighter -
which makes the clergy stronger. **The player raises the difficulty with their own hands in order to open the
door.** That is the level's whole structure in one rule, and it is a rule the game has never used.

**The counter-verb is snuffing.** Lights can be put out (the game already has snuffers, the Tollmaster's
darkness and carried lanterns). Snuff a chapel and its priests weaken - and the crypt answers.

**The priests' real threat is not the bolt: it is that they re-light what you snuffed.** That is what makes
them a target worth prioritising, and it teaches kill-the-healer-first without a tutorial line.

## The boss: the Paladin's own economy is LIGHT

The third mirror boss, and deliberately not the other two's loop - the Pyromander runs heat, the Freebooter
runs ammunition, and **the Paladin runs light**:

- **His aegis gains light from the blows it eats.** Hammering his guard pays for his own healing, which is
  exactly the trade the class lives on.
- He spends light on **mend** (healing) and **radiance columns**.
- **The loop the player drives: starve the light.** Go around the ward instead of into it - bait, riposte,
  come from behind - and punish him while he spends. Cut the mend short and he does not get the health back.
- **And the three lamps you lit to get in are lighting his arena.** Snuffing one mid-fight weakens him, at
  the cost of standing in the open to do it - so the level's mechanic is the fight's mechanic.

## The reward

Same rule as the other two optional mirror levels: clearing it **opens a coin purchase for the Paladin**, who
otherwise costs 10 silver. No bonus for players who already own him. See `the-powder-deck-pitch.md`.
